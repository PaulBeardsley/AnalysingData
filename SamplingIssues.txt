Sample Bias: Since the dataset consists of previous customers, it might not be representative of the entire population of potential loan applicants. The people who have applied for loans in the past might have different characteristics and behaviours compared to those who will apply in the future. This could lead to biased model predictions when making decisions for new loan applicants.
Temporal Changes: Economic and social conditions change over time, which can impact the likelihood of loan defaults. The dataset might not capture the latest trends, so the historical patterns observed might not hold true for new loans.
Feature Relevance: The features that were important predictors of loan default in the past might not be as relevant in the future. New factors or variables might emerge that influence loan default but are not present in the current dataset.
Data Quality and Missing Values: The dataset could have missing values, errors, or inconsistencies that could affect the performance of machine learning models. Cleaning and preprocessing the data are crucial steps in building accurate models. The reasons for missing data is important too – for example, if credit score is missing because they are a young applicant without a history or they come from overseas this might still be missing data for some new loan applicants. So we need to think carefully about how decisions are made in those cases if the usual rules won’t work with missing information.
Changing Lending Practices: Lending practices and policies of the bank might change over time based on regulatory changes, risk management strategies, and business goals. This could lead to shifts in loan approval criteria that the historical data might not reflect accurately.
Overfitting: If the model is too complex and learned the noise or idiosyncrasies of the training data, it might not generalize well to new, unseen data. In the case of human learning, this might be that they memorised the historical data for a test, but in future the topics that came up were different – scoring well on a mock that you’ve been told the topics for doesn’t mean you’d do as well in a real exam.
Ethical Concerns: If the historical data reflects biases present in the past lending decisions, using this data to make future decisions could perpetuate those biases. For example, if the historical data contains discriminatory practices, the model might inadvertently discriminate against certain groups when making loan decisions.
Concept Drift: The concept of "default" might evolve over time due to changes in regulations, industry standards, or economic conditions. Models trained on historical data might struggle to adapt to these changes.
